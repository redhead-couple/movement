## Our Purpose

This project is exploring a new medium of expression built from images, narration, sound, silence, text, timing, effects, transitions, and layered movement.

The medium is intended to grow through the ideas, experiments, and experience of the people who use and contribute to it.

For that reason, the project welcomes developers, artists, storytellers, educators, writers, designers, researchers, and people who may be contributing to an open-source project for the first time.

We want this community to be thoughtful, curious, practical, and respectful.

## Our Commitment

We are committed to providing a welcoming and constructive environment for people of different backgrounds, abilities, experience levels, cultures, identities, and perspectives.

Participation in this project should be possible without harassment, humiliation, intimidation, or personal attack.

## Expected Behavior

Participants are expected to:

- Treat other people with respect.
- Discuss ideas, code, designs, effects, transitions, and creative works without attacking the person behind them.
- Give criticism that is specific, useful, and connected to the work.
- Be willing to explain technical ideas clearly to non-technical contributors.
- Be patient with people who are learning.
- Ask questions before assuming bad intent.
- Give credit for ideas, code, visual work, documentation, and other contributions.
- Respect privacy, copyright, licenses, and confidential information.
- Disclose relevant limitations, risks, or uncertainties in a contribution.
- Accept that maintainers may request changes, postpone a proposal, or decline a contribution.
- Help preserve a space where experimentation and unusual ideas can be explored responsibly.

## Creative and Intellectual Disagreement

This project may involve difficult, abstract, emotional, philosophical, educational, or controversial ideas.

Disagreement is expected.

Contributors may strongly disagree about:

- what an effect communicates
- whether a transition has expressive value
- whether a tool belongs in the official library
- how the medium should develop
- how an example should be interpreted
- which audiences or uses should be prioritized

These disagreements should remain focused on the proposal, implementation, evidence, or creative result.

**Acceptable criticism includes:**

- explaining why a tool is confusing
- questioning whether an effect adds expressive value
- identifying technical or accessibility problems
- proposing a different interpretation
- asking for further testing
- disagreeing with a project direction

**Unacceptable behavior includes:**

- insulting a contributor
- mocking someone's ability or background
- presenting disagreement as proof that another person is dishonest or unintelligent
- repeatedly dismissing someone without engaging with their work
- using threats, intimidation, humiliation, or harassment

No contributor is required to agree with another person's interpretation of a creative tool.

## Experimentation and Failure

This project is experimental.

Ideas may fail. Effects may behave differently than expected. A contribution may be technically successful while still lacking expressive value. A promising proposal may require several attempts before it becomes useful.

Good-faith experimentation should not be treated as embarrassment or failure of character.

Contributors should be honest about what has and has not been tested.

Maintainers may classify contributions as experimental, request further testing, or decide that they are not ready for the official library.

## Effects, Transitions, and Expression Tools

Contributing an Expression Tool does not guarantee that it will become part of the official maintained library.

Review and acceptance decisions should be based on considerations such as:

- expressive usefulness
- technical reliability
- accessibility
- documentation
- performance
- licensing
- maintenance cost
- similarity to existing tools
- feedback from actual creators

These decisions should be explained respectfully.

A declined contribution may still be valuable as an experiment, community tool, or starting point for later work.

## AI-Assisted Contributions

AI tools may be used to help create code, documentation, images, sounds, examples, or other project material.

The person submitting the contribution remains fully responsible for it.

Contributors should:

- review and understand AI-generated code before submitting it
- test the result
- check for security and privacy problems
- verify factual claims
- check licensing and attribution requirements
- avoid submitting large amounts of unreviewed generated material
- disclose significant AI assistance when it is relevant to reviewing the contribution

AI-generated output should not be presented as evidence that a contribution is correct, safe, original, or legally reusable.

**Enforcement:** Submitting AI-generated material without adequate review that introduces security vulnerabilities, factual errors, or licensing violations may result in the contribution being reverted and the contributor being asked to demonstrate understanding of their own submissions before further contributions are accepted.

## Attribution and Ownership

Do not submit material that you do not have the right to contribute.

Contributors must identify third-party code, media, fonts, libraries, datasets, or other assets.

Do not remove attribution or authorship information without a valid reason.

Do not claim another person's idea or work as your own.

Questions about licensing or ownership should be raised before material is added to the official repository or public examples.

## Privacy and Confidentiality

Do not publish:

- passwords, API keys, or database credentials
- private user information
- unpublished projects or private media
- confidential communications
- contents of `server/core/app-config.php` or any deployment secrets
- security vulnerabilities that have not yet been responsibly disclosed

Security concerns should be reported through the private process described in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

Do not use private slideshow content, user data, or personal information as examples without permission.

## Unacceptable Behavior

Unacceptable behavior includes:

- harassment, threats, stalking, or intimidation
- discriminatory or degrading comments
- personal insults
- sexualized attention or imagery directed at another participant
- deliberate disruption of discussions
- repeated unwanted contact
- publishing another person's private information
- retaliation against someone who reports a concern
- plagiarism or deliberate misrepresentation of authorship
- knowingly submitting malicious code
- attempting to expose private data or security vulnerabilities publicly
- using project spaces primarily for spam, advertising, or unrelated promotion

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in official project spaces, including:

- the GitHub repository
- issues and pull requests
- GitHub Discussions
- project documentation
- official community chats or forums
- project meetings and events
- communication related to reviewing or maintaining contributions

It also applies when someone is formally representing the project in a public space.

This document does not automatically govern every independent work created with the software. Public galleries, educational programs, hosted services, or community libraries may require separate content and moderation policies.

## Reporting a Conduct Concern

Conduct concerns may be reported privately to:

**[contact@redhead-couple.org](mailto:contact@redhead-couple.org)**

Redhead Couple is currently a single-maintainer project. The current project
maintainer monitors this mailbox and reviews conduct reports. There is no
independent conduct committee or enforcement team.

Please include:

- what happened
- where it happened (link to the issue, PR, discussion, or channel)
- when it happened
- who was involved
- relevant links or screenshots
- whether there is an immediate safety or privacy concern

Reports will be handled as privately as reasonably possible.

Do not knowingly submit false or misleading reports.

Retaliation against someone who raises a good-faith concern is prohibited.

## Enforcement

The current project maintainer may take actions including:

1. A private clarification or correction.
2. A formal warning.
3. Removal of comments, contributions, or other material.
4. Temporary limits on participation.
5. Rejection of a contribution.
6. Removal from project spaces.
7. A permanent ban in serious or repeated cases.

The response should be proportionate to the behavior, its impact, and whether it continues after a warning.

Serious threats, deliberate exposure of private information, malicious code, or sustained harassment may result in immediate removal.

## Conflicts of Interest

There is currently no independent reviewer or formal escalation body. If a
report concerns the maintainer, it reaches the same maintainer; an independent
review cannot be promised.

The maintainer should acknowledge any personal involvement, consider the
report fairly, and explain the decision to the reporter while respecting
confidentiality. The prohibition on retaliation applies equally to reports
about the maintainer. Any future independent review process will be described
here only when it is actually available.

## Appeals

A person affected by an enforcement decision may request reconsideration by writing to:

**[contact@redhead-couple.org](mailto:contact@redhead-couple.org)**

Reconsideration is handled by the same current project maintainer. It is not
an appeal to an independent person, committee, or appeals board.

The request should explain why the decision may have been based on incomplete or incorrect information.

An appeal does not require the project to reopen repeated arguments that have already been reviewed.
